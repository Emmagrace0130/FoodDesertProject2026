# Literature Scan: Food Deserts in Rural Appalachia

*First pass, Oct 26, 2026. Quick web scan, not a systematic review. Check every claim against the original source before citing it.*

## What's already known

- **Definitions are contested.** USDA's measure uses straight-line distance to large stores: 1 mile (urban) or 10 to 20 miles (rural), plus income and vehicle access. Critics say it leaves out cost, store quality, and other factors. ([CRS IF11841](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF11841/IF11841.2.pdf), [ERS EIB-236](https://ers.usda.gov/sites/default/files/_laserfiche/publications/104158/EIB-236.pdf))
- **Rural evidence is mixed.** A systematic review covering 1966–2007 found mixed results on rural versus urban differences and called for studies linking access to behavior and health outcomes. ([Walker et al.; CDC PCD 2009](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2722409/)) I found no recent systematic review focused on Appalachia.
- **Dollar stores**
  - Dollar General clusters in low-income, low-access Central Appalachian counties, and residents call it a "necessary evil." ([Burroughs, VT thesis](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/items/ea51610f-19ab-41b9-bd54-2d228fa5c039))
  - Food quality is best at supermarkets, then grocery stores, then dollar stores, then convenience stores. ([Thatcher et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27862244/))
  - Dollar stores have grown fast as rural food retailers, and their food is less healthy. ([AJPH 2023](https://ajph.aphapublications.org/doi/full/10.2105/AJPH.2022.307193))
  - Independent grocers are more likely to close after a dollar store opens nearby. ([Investigate Midwest summary of a USDA study](https://investigatemidwest.org/2024/07/02/study-dollar-stores-entry-into-rural-communities-adds-to-rural-grocery-challenges/); verify against the original USDA study)
- **Grocery store closures**
  - A natural experiment in a rural Appalachian county measured how residents' shopping and diet changed after a closure. ([Gillespie et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8951354/))
  - In Alderson, WV, a study of an impending store closure found increases in gardening and food preservation, alongside more reliance on food pantries. ([RRH 3901](https://www.rrh.org.au/journal/article/3901/))
- **Cultural and informal food sources**
  - Gardening and food preservation are declining across generations. ([Batey et al. 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10901981231175360))
  - Pantry clients combine stores, pantries, and informal food sharing to get by. ([Ecol Food Nutr 2021](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/03670244.2020.1793138))
  - Hunting, fishing, and church-based food sharing appear rarely in the literature.
- **Measurement method**
  - Straight-line distance understates access barriers in rural and hilly terrain. ([PMC11995689](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11995689))
  - Measures based on driving time capture rural access better. ([GIS review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/public-health-nutrition/article/measuring-the-food-environment-using-geographical-information-systems-a-methodological-review/084217DE840179EFBC4F1CF812E6266D), [driving-time study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2214140522001931))
- **Online SNAP**
  - In 2018–19, online grocery delivery reached 31% of rural food-desert tracts, compared with 94% of urban ones. ([Yale analysis](https://medicine.yale.edu/download-file/4dc7b4d9-63d7-445c-ad3c-c6ed21caa00c/))
  - I found no update specific to Appalachia.
- **Regional and state reports**
  - [ARC: Food Insecurity in Appalachia (2023)](https://www.arc.gov/report/food-insecurity-in-appalachia/), including state profiles
  - [TN TACIR: Access to Affordable Healthy Food (2018)](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/tacir/2019publications/2018FoodDeserts.pdf)

## New developments (2025–2026)

- **USDA SNAP-authorized Retailer Access Map (SRAM)** ([documentation](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-access-research-atlas/documentation), updated Jul 27, 2026)
  - Counts all SNAP-authorized retailers, including dollar and convenience stores, using June 2025 store data.
  - Uses both straight-line and driving distance, with 2020 Census and 2020–24 ACS data, reported by census tract.
  - The old Food Access Research Atlas (FARA) is now called the Large Retailer Access Map (LRAM). It covers supermarkets only, uses straight-line distance, and relies on 2019 store data.
- **SNAP changes under H.R. 1 (P.L. 119-21)**
  - Expanded work requirements now apply to people up to age 64 and to parents of children 14 and older.
  - States must start sharing benefit costs, phased in from FY2026.
  - About 36,000 West Virginia recipients are affected.
  - Tennessee faces a projected cost shift of about $160M by FY2028.
  - Sources: [CRS R48552](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48552), [WV Public](https://wvpublic.org/story/health-science/federally-mandated-changes-to-snap-work-requirements-go-into-effect/), [Sycamore TN](https://sycamoretn.org/snap-in-tn/), [CBPP](https://www.cbpp.org/research/food-assistance/new-data-underscore-snap-cost-shifts-harm-to-low-income-families-and-state)

## Candidate gaps

1. **Do dollar stores make food deserts disappear on paper?**
   - Compare LRAM (supermarkets only) with SRAM (all SNAP stores) for Appalachian census tracts.
   - Count which tracts stop being "low access" only because a dollar or convenience store is nearby. Measure how many people that affects and what those tracts look like.
   - The SRAM is only a few months old, so almost no one has published with it yet.
2. **How much do mountains distort the measures?**
   - The SRAM reports both straight-line and driving distance.
   - Test whether the gap between them grows with terrain ruggedness.
3. **SNAP policy changes and the stores that depend on SNAP**
   - In rural places, SNAP spending helps keep grocers open.
   - Map the tracts that rely most on one or two SNAP retailers to create a baseline before the cuts take effect.
4. **Online SNAP delivery coverage in Appalachia today**
   - No update since 2018–19 data, and none specific to Appalachia.
5. **Informal food sources**
   - Hunting, fishing, gardening, and church-based food sharing are not counted in any federal measure.
   - Studying them requires qualitative or survey work, so it needs IRB approval.
